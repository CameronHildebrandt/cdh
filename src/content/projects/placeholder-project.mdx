An undergraduate capstone completed with Paul Saunders, Khalid Al-Mahrezi, and Curtis Kennedy at the University of Alberta. We investigated whether a new occlusion-aware metric could improve 3D bounding-box estimation for cars from a single RGB image.

## The question

Estimating 3D vehicle bounding boxes from a monocular image is difficult because the scene has no direct depth information. Occlusion makes it harder still: the categorical labels in the KITTI dataset do not express how much of a car is actually visible.

We proposed **Pixel Ratio**: the number of pixels belonging to the car divided by the number of pixels in its 2D bounding box. A lower ratio signals greater occlusion. Unlike raw pixel count, our analysis found the ratio had virtually no correlation with depth while remaining meaningfully correlated with occlusion.

## The approach

We used Detectron2 segmentation output and KITTI annotations to calculate Pixel Ratio for more than 20,000 vehicle samples. We then trained baseline and Pixel Ratio-weighted variants of two architectures:

- YOLO3D, a geometry-aware 3D bounding-box framework built on 2D detections.
- A ResNet-18 baseline, used to separate the effect of the metric from task-specific architecture choices.

We used a stratified 80/20 train-test split to preserve the distribution of occlusion categories and evaluated predictions with 3D IoU, average precision, coordinate/camera error, and orientation error. The project also included an implementation of rotated 3D IoU for evaluation.

## What we found

The metric was not a broad, statistically significant improvement to average precision, but it did not degrade overall model performance. The most encouraging result was a statistically significant reduction in orientation error for the Pixel Ratio YOLO3D model on highly occluded vehicles.

That makes Pixel Ratio a useful starting point rather than a finished solution: its low implementation cost and non-destructive effect suggest it could be worth testing with additional model architectures or a more precise estimate of a vehicle's unobscured area.
